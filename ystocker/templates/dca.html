The distribution is built from weekly prices divided by the statements that were public that week, so the denominator really moves — but it only steps once per filing. A five-year window is ~260 weekly points and far fewer independent fundamental readings, which is the number that bounds how much a percentile is worth. Forward figures Yahoo publishes today are shown below for context and are deliberately never ranked against this trailing distribution: for anything growing, a forward multiple is lower, so ranking one against the other reads cheap every time.

General form

Valuation sets the pace of contribution; it does not decide whether to buy.

Pmetric = percentile_rank(current vs own history)
Erel = Σ wf · Pf   (yields enter as 100 − P)
Vrel = 100 − Erel
Vdcf = 50 + c · (Σ ws · Vs − 50)
V = wdcf · Vdcf + (1 − wdcf) · Vrel
Mvaluation = 0.5 + V / 100
DCAi = Basei × Mval × Mearn × Mport

With no usable DCF, wdcf is 0 and V is the relative score alone — the missing branch is never filled with a neutral 50.
